# The grilling method of mattpocock/skills, read for the PM mode

Read on 21 November 2026, from the project's public documentation pages, by the coordinator, the session that runs agentbay for the owner. This note is written from the coordinator's summary of those pages; I fetched nothing. The project's licence is reported as MIT. No file, prompt or wording of it is used here or in the kit: the ideas below are in our own words, and a name of a concept appears as a word or two at most.

The question: what should the PM mode take from it, so that the owner's words become a requirements file in which no choice that matters is left to a guess?

## Answer in brief

1. The method is an interview that tests an idea before anyone acts on it. The person starts it on purpose. It ends when nothing important is left open and the person agrees on what was understood.
2. Its strongest ideas fit the kernel as it is: a tree of decisions, a frontier of the questions whose earlier questions are settled, all of the frontier asked at once, and a recommended answer on its own line, so a person answers with numbers and letters.
3. The idea we need most is the split between what the project's files can answer, which the interviewer finds out itself, and what only the owner can choose, which it puts to him. The stopped todo design of 21 November failed because a step was judged on a rule it had never been told. An interview that looks up what the project already says, and asks only what a person must choose, closes that gap where the requirements start.
4. Its stateful part fits too: a settled word goes into the glossary, and a decision that passes three tests is offered as an ADR. We show each one whole on a card and write it only on the owner's yes.
5. Two ideas need a change to the kernel or are left out: a free-text answer reaches the next step only if an approval hands the owner's words on (not built), and a throwaway prototype for a question talking cannot settle (the PM mode builds none).

## 1. What the method is

- **An interview, started by the person.** Its aim is to test an idea hard, until no choice that matters is left to a guess. The agent never starts one on its own.
- **A tree of decisions.** The subject is mapped as decisions. Each leads to the decisions that depend on it.
- **Rounds over the frontier.** A round asks every question whose earlier questions are settled, and never two questions where one depends on the other. Answering a round moves the frontier out. A session is a few rounds and dozens of questions. A person may ask for one question at a time.
- **No cap on questions.** A very long session means the scope is too large, and it should be split first.
- **A fixed form for a question.** A number and a title, a short body, and the interviewer's recommended answer on its own line. A person can answer a whole round with a list of numbers and short choices. The project reports a flaw: a recommendation sometimes argues against the question's own wording.
- **What the files answer, and what the person chooses.** What the files, the code or the documents can answer, the interviewer finds out itself, and only the questions that need the result wait for it. A choice belongs to the person. An interviewer that makes such a choice for him is not doing its job.
- **The end.** The frontier is empty and the person confirms the shared understanding. Nothing is acted on before.
- **The person owns the scope.** He pushes back on a shallow question, says when the talk drifts, and says "I don't know" when that is true. A question about how something should look or feel is better settled with a throwaway prototype than by a guess.
- **With documents, the interview keeps what it learns.** A term settled in the interview goes into the project's glossary at that moment, not at the end. The glossary holds the language only: one word for each idea, the words to avoid, no implementation detail. A decision is offered as an ADR only when it is hard to reverse, would surprise a later reader without its context, and was a real trade-off. The interviewer challenges a term that conflicts with the glossary, asks for a precise word where a vague one was used, tests a relationship with a concrete case, and checks the words against the code.
- **On models.** The interview leans on the model's judgement more than building does.

## 3. What we change

- **A round is a turn.** In this project's glossary a round is one pass of a slice through the test writer and the builder. One word has one meaning, so the PM mode says **turn**. This is the method's own rule, a term challenged against the glossary.
- **A free-text answer is read back.** A script cannot read free text. When his answer is not in the short form, a skill reads it, and a second card shows him, question by question, the exact words of his it took and what it will record. Nothing is kept until he confirms that card. A script checks that each excerpt is his words, exactly.
- **"At that moment" is the next turn.** A settled term is offered on the next turn's card, the first card after it is settled, and written when he says yes. It is never collected for the end.
- **The glossary keeps this project's form.** This project's glossary already names files and paths. The PM mode does not refuse them. The interviewer is told to keep a row to the meaning.
- **Signing with questions open.** The owner answered on 20 November that forge may design from a file with open questions. The sign-off card says how many are still open, and they go to `open`. The method would wait for an empty frontier; we keep his answer and show the count first.
- **The recommendation's known flaw.** The recommendation is a letter that must name one of the question's own choices, and its sentence of why stands under it. A script refuses a recommendation that names no choice. Whether the why argues against the wording is judgement; the card shows both together.
- **A long interview.** No cap on questions, but the card says how many there are so far, and past 60 it adds a line that the scope may be too large to settle in one file.

## 4. What we do not take

- **A cap on questions.** The method has no limit on questions; the kernel has budgets. Each turn is one run with its own budget, so no total is ever spent by surprise. We add no cap on questions; the earlier design's five a version is dropped.
- **A throwaway prototype.** The PM mode builds nothing. A question about how something should look or feel is marked as one talking cannot settle. It is not asked as choices. It goes to the file's `judged` sentences, which only a person judges, or stays open with the words "settle with a prototype", for forge or the owner to try.
- **An interviewer that reads everything.** The owner chose "Documents and code": the mode reads `CONTEXT.md`, `README.md`, `docs/`, `src/`, `lib/`, `app/` and `packages/`, and nothing the kernel closes to modes. A project whose code is elsewhere gives it no reach: a grant is fixed in the manifest.
- **A free loop.** The kernel has none. Each turn is one run of `ask`; its output says the command for the next turn, which runs when the owner asks.

## 5. Why

The stopped design run of 21 November (`docs/plans/0024-forge-whole.md`, "The first whole run") is the reason this matters now. The challenger judged the design not to hold P-52, a practice its designer had never been given. DA1 then gave the designers the practices held by design. The same gap is open one step earlier: what a person will judge the work on, what is left out, and what the project already decided are nowhere in the requirements unless someone asks. An interview that looks up what the project says and asks the owner what only he can decide puts those things in the file. Forge then tells its designer what the file says, and judges the design only on that.

## After the owner's answers

The owner answered the PM design's questions on 21 November (`docs/designs/pm-mode/design.md`, section 9). Two of them change what this note says we take. The interview is optional, and by default he answers each question with one of its lettered choices: answers in his own words, and so the read-back of section 3, come later, behind a setting that is off. And the requirements file carries neither his words nor the practices; his words stay in the kernel's record of each card he answered.
